# Idea stage

The idea stage is exploration: figure out what the idea is, whether it is worth pursuing, and what it could look like.

## Capture

Record the idea as soon as it appears: create the topic folder and `idea.md`, and write down the first thoughts while they are fresh.

## Exploring

Work as a thinking partner: brainstorm directions, lay them out for the user to pick from, and push back until the reasoning is solid. Stay grounded in the real code and context.

An idea can go as deep as needed: candidate directions, technical analysis, demo conclusions. Depth is the point, length is not.

Write demos and throwaway scripts to test thinking; leave production code alone.

## Research

Research is continuous. Ground the idea in what already exists: existing implementations, best practices, the current tech landscape. Early research shapes the direction; a sharper direction leads to deeper research.

Record the findings in `idea.md` with source links.

## Format

The structure of `idea.md` follows its content, and the language is free. The opening is the only required part; every other section appears when the topic needs it. Keep it logical and clear; reorganize as it grows.

- **Opening**: what the idea is and why it matters.
- **Points to do**: what to do and how. Major points get their own sections; smaller ones group into bullets.
- **Branching directions**: one section per direction with its tradeoffs; write the conclusion once decided.
- **Decisions**: the key decisions, what to do and what to skip, so later work does not relitigate them.
- **Open questions**: blockers and points still under discussion, one line each.
- **Research and references**: source links and what each source contributed; detailed findings can live here too.

## Moving on

- **Plan**: when the topic needs a plan, it is ready once you can write a solid `plan.md`. If you cannot, the idea is not mature yet; keep exploring. When ready, read `references/plan.md`.
- **Direct execution**: for simple tasks the direction converges without a plan; go straight to execution.
- **Abandon**: if the idea turns out not worth doing, record why in the idea document, then stop.
